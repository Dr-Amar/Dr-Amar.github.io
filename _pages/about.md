# About

I am **Dr. Muhammad Amar Gul**, a PhD-trained geoscientist and applied AI/ML researcher working at the intersection of **economic geology, mineral geochemistry, spatial data science, remote sensing, and mineral exploration**.

My work focuses on developing scientifically defensible methods for mineral-system discrimination, geochemical anomaly detection, prospectivity analysis, and exploration decision support. I work with geological, geochemical, geophysical, structural, remote-sensing, field, drilling, and uncertainty data, with particular emphasis on preserving geological meaning throughout the modelling workflow.

## Current work

I currently work across two complementary professional roles:

- **Senior Geoscientist (Data & AI), Erity Pty Ltd.** — research-to-product GeoAI, geochemical anomaly intelligence, target detection, multimodal exploration workflows, geostatistical outputs, and field-ready decision support.
- **Project Geologist (AI & ML), China National Geological & Mining Corporation, Saudi Arabia** — Arabian Shield prospectivity, mineral-potential modelling, remote sensing, geochemical and geophysical integration, GIS, technical screening, and exploration data systems.

I also founded and direct **[AIMEX Lab](https://aimex-geoai.github.io)**, an independent research initiative focused on artificial intelligence for mineral exploration, reproducible benchmarking, and collaborative GeoAI research.

## Research background

My PhD research at the **University of Science and Technology of China (USTC)** combined global sulfide mineral geochemistry with machine learning to investigate metallogenic discrimination, tectonic setting, ore genesis, and critical-metal enrichment. Research datasets have included pyrite, galena, and sphalerite from global ore systems, supported by trace-element geochemistry, isotopic data, geological labels, and explainable machine-learning methods.

## Scientific approach

I treat predictive modelling and geological interpretation as related but distinct tasks. My preferred workflow is:

1. establish data authority, provenance, exclusions, and QA/QC;
2. preserve geological and spatial context during feature construction;
3. use grouped, spatially appropriate, or otherwise defensible validation rather than convenient random splits;
4. quantify uncertainty and test transferability where feasible;
5. use explainability to support interpretation, not as proof of causality;
6. report negative, unstable, or non-transferable results rather than force a positive conclusion.
